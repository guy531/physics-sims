Fermi's Golden Rule: Decay of a Discrete State into a Quasi-Continuum

A discrete state \(|a\rangle\) (energy \(0\)) is coupled by a harmonic perturbation to a band of \(N\) equally spaced states \(|k\rangle\) of width \(W\) centred at \(\omega_0=1\): a quasi-continuum with level spacing \(\delta\) and density of states \(\rho=1/\delta\). The exact dynamics (diagonalising the Hamiltonian in the rotating frame) is compared with first-order perturbation theory, the golden-rule rate \(\Gamma=2\pi g^2\rho\) and exponential decay. Units: \(\hbar=1\).

Theory

Model

A state \(|a\rangle\) with \(E_a=0\) and a band \(|k\rangle\), \(E_k=\omega_0-\tfrac W2+k\delta\), \(k=0,\dots,N-1\), \(\delta=W/(N-1)\). The perturbation \(V(t)=2g\cos\omega t\) couples \(|a\rangle\) to every \(|k\rangle\). Keeping the resonant (rotating-wave) term, $$i\dot c_a=g\sum_k e^{-i\Delta_kt}c_k,\qquad i\dot c_k=g\,e^{i\Delta_kt}c_a,\qquad \Delta_k=E_k-E_a-\omega .$$ In the rotating frame the Hamiltonian is time independent, so the exact solution follows from its eigenvalues \(\lambda\), the roots of \(\lambda=\sum_k g^2/(\lambda-\Delta_k)\).

First order

$$|c_k^{(1)}(t)|^2=4g^2\,\frac{\sin^2(\Delta_kt/2)}{\Delta_k^2},\qquad P_a^{(1)}=1-\sum_k|c_k^{(1)}|^2 .$$ For \(t\gg2\pi/W\) the peak (height \(g^2t^2\), width \(\sim2\pi/t\)) fits inside the band and \(\frac{\sin^2(\Delta t/2)}{\Delta^2}\to\frac{\pi t}{2}\delta(\Delta)\). Replacing \(\sum_k\to\int\rho\,dE\):

Fermi's golden rule

$$P_{out}(t)=\Gamma t,\qquad \Gamma=\frac{2\pi}{\hbar}\,|V_{ka}|^2\,\rho(E_a+\hbar\omega).$$ A constant transition rate, even though every single \(|c_k|^2\) oscillates. Valid when $$\frac{2\pi\hbar}{W}\ll t\ll\frac1\Gamma,\ t_H,\qquad\text{i.e.}\quad \delta\ll\hbar\Gamma\ll W .$$

Beyond first order

Wigner–Weisskopf: the exact survival probability is \(P_a=e^{-\Gamma t}\) and the final distribution is the Lorentzian \(|c_k(\infty)|^2=g^2/(\Delta_k^2+\Gamma^2/4)\): the decaying level acquires the width \(\hbar\Gamma\).
Short times (\(t\lesssim2\pi/W\)): \(P_{out}\approx Ng^2t^2\), quadratic, not linear.
Recurrence: a discrete band has a finite memory. At \(t_H=2\pi\rho\) all phases \(e^{-ik\delta t}\) realign and population returns to \(|a\rangle\).

Things to try

  • Golden-rule regime: the exact curve follows e−Γt, while first order and 1 − Γt agree only for t ≪ 1/Γ. Switch on the log axis: the exact decay is a straight line.
  • Short times: the decay starts quadratically. The rate panel shows how the rate builds up from 0 to Γ over a time ~2π/W.
  • Double g: does Γ change by a factor 4? Double N at fixed W (ρ doubles): what happens to Γ? To keep the physics fixed as N → ∞, how must g scale?
  • Few levels: watch the revival at tH = 2πρ. Increase N and the revival moves out: a true continuum has no memory.
  • Watch the distribution panel: the sinc² shrinks as 2π/t until its width reaches Γ, then it freezes into a Lorentzian.
  • Resolved levels: with Γ < δ only one level is resonant and the system Rabi-oscillates. There is no decay and no golden rule.
  • Move ω out of the band (ρ = 0, no decay), or close to a band edge. What does the golden rule predict there, and what really happens?
  • Strong coupling: Γ comparable to W. The decay is no longer exponential and part of the population stays in |a⟩ (a bound state forms outside the band).
