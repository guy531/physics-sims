Theory

Setup

\(H=H_0+V(t)\), with \(H_0|a\rangle=E_a|a\rangle,\ H_0|b\rangle=E_b|b\rangle\), \(\omega_0=(E_b-E_a)/\hbar\). Write $$|\psi(t)\rangle=c_a(t)e^{-iE_at/\hbar}|a\rangle+c_b(t)e^{-iE_bt/\hbar}|b\rangle .$$

Exact equations (solved numerically here)

$$i\hbar\,\dot c_a=V_{ab}(t)\,e^{-i\omega_0t}\,c_b,\qquad i\hbar\,\dot c_b=V_{ba}(t)\,e^{i\omega_0t}\,c_a$$ with \(c_a(0)=1,\ c_b(0)=0\). Here \(V_{ab}=V_{ba}=V_0\,g(t)\cos\omega t\).

First order

Set \(c_a\approx1\) on the right-hand side: $$c_b^{(1)}(t)=-\frac{i}{\hbar}\int_0^t V_{ba}(t')\,e^{i\omega_0t'}\,dt' .$$ Valid as long as \(|c_b|^2\ll1\), i.e. the initial state is hardly depleted.

Harmonic perturbation

Keeping only the resonant term (\(\Delta=\omega-\omega_0\)): $$P_b^{(1)}(t)\approx\frac{V_0^2}{\hbar^2}\,\frac{\sin^2(\Delta t/2)}{\Delta^2}.$$ At resonance \(P_b^{(1)}=V_0^2t^2/4\hbar^2\) grows without bound, so first order requires \(V_0t/\hbar\ll1\). For large \(t\), \(\sin^2(\Delta t/2)/\Delta^2\to\frac{\pi t}{2}\,\delta(\Delta)\): a rate, i.e. Fermi's golden rule.

Exact (rotating-wave) result: Rabi oscillations

$$P_b(t)=\frac{V_0^2}{V_0^2+\hbar^2\Delta^2}\,\sin^2\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{V_0^2+\hbar^2\Delta^2}}{2\hbar}\,t\right)$$ Expanding for small \(V_0\) reproduces the first-order formula.

Several levels

Levels \(|1\rangle,\dots,|N\rangle\) (with two levels, \(|a\rangle=|1\rangle,\ |b\rangle=|2\rangle\)), \(\omega_{nm}=(E_n-E_m)/\hbar\), start in \(|1\rangle\): $$i\hbar\,\dot c_n=\sum_m V_{nm}(t)\,e^{i\omega_{nm}t}\,c_m .$$ First order only reaches levels coupled directly to \(|1\rangle\): \(c_n^{(1)}=-\frac i\hbar\int_0^tV_{n1}e^{i\omega_{n1}t'}dt'\), with a resonance at \(\omega=\omega_{n1}\) for each such level. A level reached only through an intermediate \(|m\rangle\) needs second order, $$c_n^{(2)}=\Big(\!-\frac i\hbar\Big)^{\!2}\sum_m\int_0^t\!dt'\,V_{nm}(t')e^{i\omega_{nm}t'}\int_0^{t'}\!dt''\,V_{m1}(t'')e^{i\omega_{m1}t''},$$ which is resonant when \(2\omega=\omega_{n1}\) (two-photon absorption), with strength \(\propto V_0^2/(\omega_{m1}-\omega)\).

Ladders and the harmonic oscillator

With equal spacing every step of the ladder is resonant at the same \(\omega\), so the drive climbs the whole ladder. Anharmonicity detunes the upper steps and the system stays almost a two-level system. With couplings \(\propto\sqrt n\) (the matrix elements of \(x\)) the system is a driven harmonic oscillator: the exact state is a coherent state, the populations are Poissonian with mean \(\bar n=(V_0t/2\hbar)^2\), and the first-order \(P_2^{(1)}\) is exactly \(\bar n\).

Gaussian pulse, ω = 0

For \(V=V_0e^{-(t-t_0)^2/2\tau^2}\): $$P_b^{(1)}=\frac{2\pi V_0^2\tau^2}{\hbar^2}\,e^{-\omega_0^2\tau^2}.$$ Short pulse (\(\omega_0\tau\ll1\)): sudden, transitions happen. Long pulse (\(\omega_0\tau\gg1\)): adiabatic, the system follows and returns to \(|a\rangle\).

Switching on a constant V: sudden vs. adiabatic

Envelope \(g=0\) for \(t<t_{on}\), then \(g=\sin^2\!\big(\pi(t-t_{on})/2T\big)\) up to \(t_{on}+T\), then \(g=1\) (\(T=0\): a sudden step). With \(V\) on, the eigenstates of \(H_0+V\) are \(|a'\rangle\approx|a\rangle-\frac{V_0}{\hbar\omega_0}|b\rangle\) and \(|b'\rangle\). Sudden (\(\omega_0T\ll1\)): the state is still \(|a\rangle=\) a superposition of \(|a'\rangle,|b'\rangle\), which beat: $$P_b\approx\frac{4V_0^2}{\hbar^2\omega_0^2}\sin^2\frac{\omega_0(t-t_{on})}{2}.$$ Adiabatic (\(\omega_0T\gg1\)): the state follows \(|a'\rangle\), so \(P_b\to V_0^2/\hbar^2\omega_0^2\) with no oscillation.

Things to try

  • Weak, resonant → then slowly raise V₀. At what value of V₀t does first order start to fail? Watch the orange ring outgrow the level.
  • Off resonance: move ω away from 1. The oscillation period of Pb is 2π/|Δ| and its amplitude ~ V₀²/Δ². In the complex plane cb runs on a circle.
  • Pause at several times and look at the line shape: the central peak height grows as t², the width shrinks as 1/t, so the area grows as t.
  • π-pulse: the exact solution transfers the whole population to |b⟩, first order predicts P > 2. What pulse area gives a 2π-pulse?
  • Sudden vs. slow pulse: change τ with ω = 0 and compare with \(2\pi V_0^2\tau^2e^{-\tau^2}\).
  • Constant V, sudden: even a static perturbation produces small, fast oscillations of Pb at frequency ω₀ once it is switched on. Why don't they grow?
  • Constant V, adiabatic: now increase the ramp time T from 0 to ~40. The oscillations die out and Pb settles at V₀²/ω₀². Around which value of ω₀T does the crossover happen?
  • Two lines: two upper levels, both coupled to |1⟩. The line shape of the target has a peak at its own frequency; switch the target to see the other one.
  • 3-level ladder: |3⟩ is not coupled to |1⟩, so first order says P₃ = 0 forever, but the exact solution climbs to |3⟩. How does P₃ grow at short times (t², t⁴, …)?
  • Anharmonic ladder: the upper steps are detuned and the population stays in |1⟩, |2⟩. Press "Equal spacing" and watch it climb.
  • Two-photon resonance: ω is detuned from |2⟩ but 2ω = ω₃₁. Look for the extra peak at ω₃₁/2 in the line shape of |3⟩ (exact only).
  • Harmonic oscillator: the populations of |1⟩…|6⟩ form a Poisson distribution; compare P₂(1) with the mean excitation. Try adding anharmonicity.
