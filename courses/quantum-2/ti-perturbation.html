Time-Independent Perturbation Theory: Energy Levels as the Perturbation Grows

An unperturbed system \(H_0\) (harmonic oscillator or infinite well) gets a perturbation \(\lambda W\). As \(\lambda\) grows, the exact levels (diagonalising \(H_0+\lambda W\)) are compared with first-order, second-order and K-th order perturbation theory. Units: \(\hbar=m=1\); oscillator \(\omega=1\); well width \(L=\pi\).

Theory

The setup

\(H=H_0+\lambda W\), where the unperturbed problem is solved: \(H_0|n^{(0)}\rangle=E_n^{(0)}|n^{(0)}\rangle\), non-degenerate. Expand $$E_n=E_n^{(0)}+\lambda E_n^{(1)}+\lambda^2E_n^{(2)}+\dots,\qquad |n\rangle=|n^{(0)}\rangle+\lambda|n^{(1)}\rangle+\dots$$ and match powers of \(\lambda\) in \(H|n\rangle=E_n|n\rangle\).

First order

$$E_n^{(1)}=W_{nn}=\langle n^{(0)}|W|n^{(0)}\rangle,\qquad |n^{(1)}\rangle=\sum_{m\ne n}\frac{W_{mn}}{E_n^{(0)}-E_m^{(0)}}\,|m^{(0)}\rangle .$$ The energy shift is the average of the perturbation in the unperturbed state. The state mixes in its neighbours, most strongly those close in energy.

Second order

$$E_n^{(2)}=\sum_{m\ne n}\frac{|W_{mn}|^2}{E_n^{(0)}-E_m^{(0)}} .$$ Levels above push \(n\) down, levels below push it up: level repulsion. The ground state is always lowered at second order.

When is it valid?

All corrections are expansions in $$\left|\frac{\lambda W_{mn}}{E_n^{(0)}-E_m^{(0)}}\right|\ll1 .$$ The badge in the sidebar shows the largest such ratio for the chosen level.

Higher orders and the exact answer

With intermediate normalisation \(\langle n^{(0)}|n^{(k)}\rangle=0\), $$E_n^{(k)}=\langle n^{(0)}|W|n^{(k-1)}\rangle,\qquad \langle m^{(0)}|n^{(k)}\rangle=\frac{\langle m^{(0)}|W|n^{(k-1)}\rangle-\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}E_n^{(j)}\langle m^{(0)}|n^{(k-j)}\rangle}{E_n^{(0)}-E_m^{(0)}} .$$ Here this recursion and the exact diagonalisation both use the same 40 unperturbed states, so the two are compared fairly.

Worked examples

Oscillator + λx: completing the square, \(E_n=n+\tfrac12-\tfrac{\lambda^2}{2}\) exactly. So \(E^{(1)}=0\), \(E^{(2)}=-\tfrac12\), and all higher orders vanish.
Oscillator + λx²/2: \(\omega\to\sqrt{1+\lambda}\), so \(E_n=(n+\tfrac12)\sqrt{1+\lambda}\). This binomial series converges only for \(|\lambda|<1\).
Oscillator + λx⁴: \(E_0=\tfrac12+\tfrac34\lambda-\tfrac{21}{8}\lambda^2+\tfrac{333}{16}\lambda^3-\dots\) The coefficients grow factorially and the series diverges for every \(\lambda\neq0\): for \(\lambda<0\) the potential has no bound states (Dyson's argument). It is an asymptotic series.
Well + uniform field: \(E^{(1)}=0\) by parity, so the shift starts at \(\lambda^2\).

Things to try

  • Oscillator in a field: first order gives no shift at all, and second order is already exact for every level. Raise K: nothing changes. Look at the wave function: it is just displaced.
  • Stiffer spring: the series works up to λ = 1 and then fails, no matter how large K is. In the order-by-order panel, compare λ = 0.8 with λ = 1.4.
  • Anharmonic x⁴ at small λ: first order is good, second order much better. Now try the strong preset and raise K from 2 to 24. Adding terms first helps, then makes things worse: an asymptotic series.
  • Second order always lowers the ground state. Check this for every preset with the ground state selected. Which levels go up?
  • Well in a field: why is the first-order line flat? Which levels repel each other most strongly, and why?
  • Barrier in the middle: the levels with n = 2, 4 hardly move. Look at their wave functions at x = L/2. What do the lowest two levels do at large λ?
  • Watch the admixture bars: which m mix in for each perturbation? Explain from parity, and from \(x=(a+a^\dagger)/\sqrt2\) for the oscillator.
  • Compare the badge value with the actual error in the readouts. Is the criterion |λWmn/ΔE| ≪ 1 a good guide?
